# Siftwell user guide

## Quick start

1. **Download.** On the project's **Releases** page, open the latest release. On Windows take **Siftwell-Setup-x.y.z.exe**; on a Mac made since late 2020 take **Siftwell-x.y.z.dmg**. (Not sure? See **Which download do I want?** below.)
2. **Install and open it.** Windows: double-click the file, then **Next**, **Install**, **Finish**. Mac: open the file and drag **Siftwell** onto **Applications**, then open it from there. The first time, your computer may ask whether you trust it: the steps are under [Install](#install).
3. **Add yourself** and answer the short setup (about five minutes): your resume or a short form, your home ZIP, how far you'd travel, pay and level. Everything except your name can be skipped.
4. **Press Start my job search** on the last screen. Siftwell shows how many employers it found near you and how long checking them takes, then gets to work. The first jobs appear on your board within a few minutes.
5. **Use your board.** Open a job to read it, then **Save**, **Mark applied** or **Not for me**. Press **Find new jobs** (the circular-arrow icon) whenever you want to look again.

That's all you need. Claude (for Match ratings and resume tailoring) is optional and can be connected any time in **Settings → Get started**. Everything stays on your computer.

**Which download do I want?**

| You have | Download |
|---|---|
| A Windows PC | **Siftwell-Setup-x.y.z.exe** (the installer) |
| A Mac with an Apple chip (M1 or newer, late 2020 onward) | **Siftwell-x.y.z.dmg** |
| A Windows PC where you can't install programs, or a USB stick | **Siftwell-Portable-x.y.z.zip** ([how](ADVANCED.md#portable-version-no-install)) |
| A Mac with an Intel chip | **Siftwell-x.y.z.zip** ([how](ADVANCED.md#intel-mac-or-advanced-use-the-zip-works-on-windows-too)) |

The rest of this guide explains every page and setting. Less common things (the portable version, the ZIP, uninstalling, where files are kept, troubleshooting, setting things up by hand) are in **[More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md)**.

## What it does

- **Goes straight to the source.** Siftwell reads job listings directly from employers' own career sites (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters, SAP SuccessFactors, Taleo, Radancy, Eightfold, Avature, and school-district and hospital systems such as AppliTrack/Frontline, SchoolSpring and Jobvite, and 13 more), including smaller and nonprofit employers whose jobs often never reach the big job sites.
- **Filters to what fits you.** Distance from your home ZIP code, remote or in person, pay floor, seniority, travel, security clearance, and degree or license requirements you don't meet.
- **Two scores for every job:**
  - **Relevant:** how close the job is to the work you do (your job titles, other names for them, related roles, your skills). Siftwell works this out itself for every job; no Claude needed.
  - **Appeal:** how good the job is for *you* (pay, location, level, company, travel).
  - **Match:** how well your resume fits what *they* want, judged by Claude.
- **Search in plain English.** Type "remote product roles at climate nonprofits over 150k" and press Enter.
- **Reads your resume.** Upload a PDF, Word file or even a phone photo, and Siftwell builds your profile from it. You review it before it's used.
- **Tailors your resume** for a specific job, as a PDF or Word file, and asks you questions when it needs facts your resume doesn't have.
- **Optional second opinion ("Astra").** A ChatGPT-based reviewer grades each tailored resume and sends it back for another pass if it falls short.
- **Career "Target" tab.** Describe where you want to go next ("an AI product role", "move into nonprofit leadership") and get jobs rated for that goal, with what to work on.
- **Household profiles.** Each person has their own resume, settings, saved jobs and tailored resumes.
- **Use it from your phone.** Open the board from any phone or laptop on your home Wi-Fi using a private link.

## Install

No terminal and no administrator password needed. (The portable version, the ZIP and uninstalling are in [More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md).)

### Windows: use the installer (recommended)
1. On this page, open **Releases** (on the right side, or under the repository name on a phone), click the **latest** release, and under **Assets** download **Siftwell-Setup-x.y.z.exe** (for example `Siftwell-Setup-0.1.0.exe`).
2. Double-click it, click **Next**, then **Install**, then **Finish**. Siftwell opens.

That's all: it installs just for you, adds **Siftwell** to your Start menu (tick the box on the first page if you also want a desktop shortcut) and lists itself under **Settings → Apps**. Python and everything else Siftwell needs are already inside, so there is no further download. Then carry on at **First run** below.

**One-time security prompt.** The installer isn't digitally signed yet, so Windows may show **"Windows protected your PC"**: click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. (If you see "Open File – Security Warning" instead, click **Run**.) A "Windows Security" firewall window may also ask about Siftwell: click **Allow** if you want to open Siftwell from your phone, otherwise **Cancel** (Siftwell still works on this computer either way).

**Updating.** Siftwell tells you when a newer version is out and can install it for you (see **Updating Siftwell** below). Or download and run the newer **Siftwell-Setup-x.y.z.exe** the same way. It closes Siftwell if it is running, replaces the program, and your profiles, ratings and settings are exactly as you left them.

### Mac (Apple silicon): use the .dmg (recommended)
This is the normal way to install Siftwell on a Mac made since late 2020 (an M1, M2, M3 or newer chip; **Apple menu → About This Mac** says "Chip: Apple M…") running macOS 13 or newer. No Terminal, no Python install and no administrator password needed. (A Mac with an Intel chip uses the ZIP: see [More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md#intel-mac-or-advanced-use-the-zip-works-on-windows-too).)
1. On this page, open **Releases** (on the right side, or under the repository name on a phone), click the **latest** release, and under **Assets** download **Siftwell-x.y.z.dmg** (for example `Siftwell-0.4.0.dmg`).
2. Open the downloaded file (double-click it in your Downloads folder). A window shows the **Siftwell** app next to an **Applications** folder. **Drag Siftwell onto Applications.**
3. Close that window, then eject the disk image (click the eject button next to **Siftwell** in the Finder sidebar) and delete the .dmg if you like.
4. Open **Siftwell** from **Applications** (or Launchpad or Spotlight). Siftwell opens in its own window, and Python and everything else it needs are already inside the app, so there is no further download. Then carry on at **First run** below.

**One-time security prompt.** Siftwell isn't from the App Store and isn't signed with an Apple developer certificate yet, so the first time your Mac says something like *"Siftwell cannot be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software"* (or *"cannot be opened because it is from an unidentified developer"*, or *"Siftwell is damaged"* on some versions):
- **macOS 14 or older:** click **Cancel** (or **Done**), then in **Applications** **right-click** (or Control-click) **Siftwell**, choose **Open**, and click **Open** again.
- **macOS 15 (Sequoia) or newer:** the right-click trick no longer works. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the message about "Siftwell" and click **Open Anyway**, then confirm with your password or Touch ID and click **Open Anyway** again.
- Only the first start asks. Updates that Siftwell installs for you don't ask again.

The icon in the Dock is Siftwell; closing the window (or right-clicking the Dock icon → **Quit**) stops it. If **Keep Siftwell available to my phone when the window is closed** is on, a small Siftwell icon stays in the menu bar at the top of the screen after you close the window (it has **Open Siftwell**, **Copy phone link** and **Stop Siftwell**). If the first-launch prompt blocks you for another reason, the ZIP is the fallback ([More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md)).

**Updating.** Siftwell tells you when a newer version is out and can install it for you (see **Updating Siftwell** below). Or download the newer **Siftwell-x.y.z.dmg**, quit Siftwell, and drag the new Siftwell onto **Applications** again, choosing **Replace**. Your profiles, ratings and settings are exactly as you left them.

## First run

### 1. Follow the page in the Siftwell window
Siftwell opens in its own window; it runs entirely on your computer. Links to other websites, such as job postings and the Claude sign-in page, open in your normal web browser, and resume downloads show a normal Save dialog. (If your computer can't show the Siftwell window, Siftwell says so and opens in your web browser instead; see [More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md#troubleshooting).) You'll be asked to add the first person, then a short setup walks you through your resume, location, pay and goals, and ends with **Start my job search** (below).

A few things about the setup questions: your name is asked once (if you already gave it on the welcome page, setup just greets you, with **Use a different name** underneath). **Back** always takes you to the previous screen, and nothing is chosen for you. **Pay** is optional: leave the boxes blank and you see jobs at any pay (you can add a lowest base later in Filters). The **Your work** screen asks for your **most recent job title** (already filled in if Siftwell found it in your resume or profile; change it if it's off) and your level. The title is what Siftwell uses to tell which jobs are close to your work, so it reminds you once if you leave it blank; you can still carry on without one. **Level** runs from Entry level to VP / Executive; pick yours, or **Show me every level**. Siftwell has no ready-made lists of companies or industries and no built-in list of job titles: the **Employers** screen explains that your employers come from the Siftwell employer directory near you, the companies you add yourself, and (optionally) what Claude finds, and that Siftwell checks their job pages when you press **Find new jobs**, or by itself every day if you turn that on in Settings (it starts off). On the **Dealbreakers** screen, travel, employers and industries to skip come first; security clearance is last, and only matters if government or defense jobs come up in your search. If you filled in your profile yourself instead of uploading a resume, the next screen simply shows the profile you typed.

**The last setup step: Start my job search.** Siftwell doesn't look for jobs until you tell it to. The last screen of setup lists what will happen, and nothing runs until you press **Start my job search**:
1. **Add employers near you from the Siftwell directory** (free, no Claude): the screen says how many employers the [Siftwell employer directory](#the-siftwell-employer-directory) has with openings within your radius, and lets you choose: **all** of them, or only the 100, 250, 500 or 1,000 **with the most openings near you**. Under each choice it says about how many openings that is, how long the first check takes and how long looking for new jobs takes each day afterwards (worked out from the real number of employers). Near a big city the choice of 500 is picked for you; you can add the rest later in **Settings → Employer directory**. The employers you choose become **your employers**, the ones your board watches. If you chose remote work only, it offers the employers with the most remote jobs in the US instead. It needs your home ZIP (unless you chose remote only). **If the directory can't be reached** (you're offline), the screen says so plainly before you start: Siftwell then begins with a starter list of about 200 large US employers that comes with the app, checks the ones most likely to hire in your state first, and afterwards keeps only the ones with jobs near you (or remote jobs, if you're open to remote work). Your board and **Settings → Employer directory** keep saying that this is the starter list, with **Try the directory again**.
2. **Find more employers with Claude** (optional, only if you tick the box; needs Claude): Claude also searches the web for about 80 employers within your radius that the directory may not have (big employers, hospitals, universities, government, nonprofits, startups and more), using your Claude plan, and Siftwell checks which of their careers pages it can read. Those go on your **Near me** list too. This takes about 20 to 40 minutes, so the box starts unticked; the directory is usually enough to start.
3. **Check career pages for openings:** Siftwell visits the job pages of the employers you already watch, plus the ones it just added, starting with the ones that have the most openings near you. You don't have to wait for all of it: the first jobs reach your board within a few minutes, and it keeps filling in every few minutes after that. A few very large employers can take much longer to read than the rest; Siftwell doesn't wait for them, carries on with the others, and finishes those at the end.
4. **Keep the jobs that fit** your area, your pay floor and level (if you gave them), minus your dealbreakers.
5. **Rate the best matches** (only when Claude is connected): Claude reads the jobs most relevant to your work against your resume, using your Claude plan: up to 500 jobs in one go. The rest show **—** for Match until you press **Rate more** on your board.

After you press the button, the same screen shows a checklist with each step's progress (for example "500 employers added", then "Checking employers: 184 of 500 · your board fills in as we go"), and **Go to my board**, which you can press any time. That one line is the same wherever you look (this screen, your board, Settings, the Employers page), and the number only goes up. If the search ends with nothing on your board, the screen says why (for example how many open jobs your employers have and which part of your search none of them matched) and your board lists what usually helps. If the check stops before it's done (you paused it, closed Siftwell or restarted the computer), nothing is lost: your board says how far it got and offers **Continue checking**. You can leave the screen while it runs; the search keeps going as long as Siftwell is running (closing the Siftwell window stops Siftwell, see **Starting and stopping**). Not ready? Press **Skip for now**: your board then shows a **Start your job search** card instead (see **Using it day to day**). Without Claude, the directory step works the same; the screen just mentions that Claude (optional) can also search the web for more employers, with one **Connect Claude (optional)** link. If there are no employers to check at all and no home ZIP to look near, the screen says so and offers **Back to Employers** and **Add a company by link** instead of the button. Open setup again after your search has started and the last screen simply says your answers are saved, with **Go to my board**.

**Connecting Claude during setup.** If Claude isn't connected yet, the resume step shows a **Connect Claude** box, so you can do it right there without leaving setup. Claude Code is a free app from Anthropic that lets Siftwell use your Claude account; installing it is free, using it needs a paid Claude plan (such as Pro or Max). The box shows only what's still needed:
- **Not installed:** click **Install Claude Code** (Siftwell runs the official installer; it takes a minute or two), then sign in.
- **Installed but not signed in:** click **Sign in to Claude**. The Claude sign-in page opens in your normal web browser (if it doesn't, copy the link the box shows into your browser). Sign in, copy the code the page shows, paste it into the box and click **Finish sign-in**.
- **Already connected:** the box says so and the step goes straight to uploading your resume.

When Claude is connected, the step switches to **Add your resume** and Claude reads it for you. If you installed Claude Code or signed in from a terminal instead, come back to the Siftwell window (or click **Check again**) and it notices. Don't want Claude? Click **Skip Claude and fill in a short form** (see **Using Siftwell without Claude** below); you can connect it later. After the resume step the full box isn't shown again: the **Direction** step, the tailoring-questions step and the last screen each have one small **Connect Claude** link that opens it. The welcome page also has a **Connect Claude first (optional)** button that opens the **Get started** checklist.

### 2. Finish setup with the "Get started" checklist (optional)
Open **Get started** (linked from the welcome page; once you have a profile it is the first item in **Settings**, the gear icon at the top, and the gear shows a small number while required steps are missing). It checks your computer and fixes things with buttons:
- **Browser for resume PDFs:** found automatically (Edge is already on Windows).

**Claude (optional).** Connect Claude to unlock Match ratings, resume reading and tailoring; Siftwell works without it. These two buttons never count as missing steps:
- **Claude Code installed:** click **Install Claude Code** and Siftwell runs the official installer for you.
- **Signed in to Claude:** click **Sign in to Claude**, open the link, sign in with your Claude account, and paste the code it shows back into Siftwell.

Optional switches on the same page: **Start Siftwell when I sign in to my computer** (on Windows it also offers **Switch to the new startup** if an older setup is still starting Siftwell), **Use Siftwell on my phone** (shows a link and a QR code to scan), **Keep Siftwell available to my phone when the window is closed** (see below), and how to set up **Astra** resume reviews (optional: install OpenAI's Codex app and sign in with your ChatGPT account).

### 3. Add more employers (optional)
A new install starts with no companies. **Start my job search** at the end of setup adds employers near you from the free [Siftwell employer directory](#the-siftwell-employer-directory) (no Claude needed). To add more, open the **Employers** page (the database icon at the top, next to Re-rate), stay on its **Employers** section, and paste the link to a company's careers page under **Add an employer** (a bare address such as `careers.example.com` is fine; Siftwell adds the `https://`). Siftwell works out how the employer lists its jobs, checks that it can read them, adds it, and gets its jobs right away. Besides the common job systems (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Taleo, Radancy, Eightfold, Avature, AppliTrack (Frontline) and SchoolSpring for school districts, Jobvite and others; for a company on SuccessFactors or Taleo you can paste its careers page or its job-search page, and Siftwell finds the job system behind it; a very large Avature site may show as **Too many to read all** on the Employers page because only its first few hundred jobs are read, and a site that forbids automated reading is left alone and shows **Couldn't be read**), Siftwell can also read many custom careers sites that publish standard job data (the markup Google Jobs uses); if a site still can't be read, Siftwell tells you so when you paste the link. (You can still add a company from **My profile → Companies** too.) For safety Siftwell only opens ordinary public web links (`https://` or `http://`): it refuses links to this computer or to devices on your home network, and links on unusual ports.

Already set up, or want the directory's newest employers now? Use **Settings → Employer directory** (it shows how many it would add and what that costs in time before you press the button; the same offer appears on an empty board). Want even more? **Employers → Find employers near me** (see **The Employers page** below): Claude looks up employers in your area and Siftwell checks which ones it can read, so you can add them with a tick.

## Using it day to day

- **Starting and stopping:** double-click **Start Siftwell** any time; it opens the Siftwell window (if it's already running, it just opens a window on it, or brings the open one to the front). **Closing the Siftwell window stops Siftwell**, including finding new jobs automatically when that is set to **While Siftwell is running**, so leave the window open (minimized is fine) if you want it to keep working in the background. (Set to **Even when Siftwell is closed**, it still runs; see **Find new jobs automatically**.) If Siftwell is finding new jobs or re-rating, it asks before it stops. The exception is **Keep Siftwell available to my phone when the window is closed** in **Settings → Get started** (off by default): when it's on, Siftwell keeps running in the background after you close the window (a little memory used, reachable on your home network; a Siftwell icon appears in the system tray near the clock, and right-clicking it lets you open Siftwell, copy the phone link, or stop Siftwell) until you turn the switch off, double-click **Stop Siftwell**, or restart your computer, so your phone and the automatic daily look for new jobs keep working, and **Start Siftwell** later just opens a window again. To stop it completely at any time, double-click **Stop Siftwell** (it also closes the window). **Stop Siftwell** only ends the Siftwell that was started from this copy, which it finds from the note Siftwell keeps in your data folder; it never ends other programs, and if it finds no Siftwell it just says "Siftwell isn't running". If Siftwell was started some other way (for example by an older Windows scheduled task), restart your computer or sign out and back in. If Siftwell was started by **Start Siftwell when I sign in**, it is already running when you open a window, and closing the window leaves it running. Siftwell remembers the window's size and position.
- **An empty board** tells you what to do next, at the top of the **Jobs** tab:
  - **Start your job search** (if you haven't started one yet, for example after **Skip for now**): what it will do, how many employers the Siftwell directory has near you with the same choice as in setup (all, or the ones with the most openings, each with its time estimate), an optional **Also find more employers … with Claude** box (when Claude is connected; unticked), and the **Start my job search** button. It runs the same steps as the end of setup. If you haven't given a job title yet, it also suggests adding one first.
  - **Your job search is running**, with what it is doing right now in one plain line (for example "Checking employers: 184 of 1,043 · your board fills in as we go"), a progress bar and a **Pause** button. This is the only progress display on the page. The page shows your jobs as soon as the first ones are in, usually within a few minutes; from then on a single line at the top of the board keeps showing the same progress, with **Show new jobs** when more have arrived and **Pause**. The number never goes backwards, and it is the same number on every page. If Siftwell itself has stopped (you closed it, or the computer restarted), the page says **Siftwell isn't running** instead of going on showing progress.
  - **No jobs that fit yet** or **Your employers haven't been checked yet** (the first check stopped part-way: you paused it, or Siftwell was closed): how many employers have been checked so far and a **Continue checking** button, which carries on where it stopped. When your board already has jobs, the same offer is a line at the top: "Siftwell has checked 184 of your 1,043 employers. 859 still to check." with **Continue checking** and **Not now**. **Find new jobs** also checks whatever is left.
  - **No jobs matched your search** (every employer was checked, but nothing fits): one sentence on why (for example "Your 214 employers have 3,880 open jobs, but none matched your search (within 35 miles of Columbus, OH or remote; with a job title like yours)"), then what usually helps, the most useful first: **Add them** next to the number of further employers the directory has for your area (with the same choice and time estimates as above), or **Try the directory again** if it couldn't be reached; **Add more job titles** (or **Tell Siftwell what work you do** if you gave none); **Widen your search** (opens **Filters → My search**: a bigger radius, remote work, a lower pay floor; after you widen it, the card offers the employers in the wider area); **Find more employers with Claude**; and **Add an employer by its careers link**.
- **No job title yet?** A board without one keeps every kind of job and can only order them by pay, place and level, so a line at the top says **Tell Siftwell what work you do to see the most relevant jobs first**, with **Add my job title** (it opens **My profile → Job search**) and **Not now**.
- **The board** shows your matching jobs with **Relevant**, **Appeal** and **Match** scores. **Relevant** (0 to 100) is how close a job is to the work you do; open a job to see why in plain words ("Matches “Staff Nurse” (another name for your job); mentions patient care"). See **What Siftwell looks for** below. Click a job for Claude's read on it, then **Save**, **Mark applied**, **Not for me**, or **Tailor my resume**. On a computer the job's details open beside the list, just below the search bar, and scroll on their own, so you can read the whole listing wherever you are in the list; on a phone they open full screen (**‹ All jobs** goes back).
- **Pay, level and workplace on a job** come from the posting itself, read by plain code (no Claude):
  - **Pay** is shown the way the posting states it: an hourly job says **$36.90/hr** or **$18.50–$22.75/hr**, a salaried one **$45k–$52k**; a single figure ("Starting at $19.25 per hour", "$40,000 per year") counts too, and pay written in the job title is read as well. To compare jobs (your lowest base, **Highest pay** sorting, the **Salary listed** box, Appeal), hourly pay is counted as a full-time year of 2,080 hours, and the job's details say so ("about $77k a year full time"). When a posting lists several pay ranges (one per level, or per location), Siftwell goes by the highest one, so your lowest base never hides a job that could pay enough. Sign-on bonuses, 401(k) matches, tuition help, shift differentials, total or on-target earnings next to a base range, fundraising goals and company revenue figures are not pay and are ignored. The **pay bar** in a job's details is drawn on your own board's range of pay (stretched to include your lowest base and current pay), so a $45k or $20/hr job is as readable as a $300k one.
  - **Level** comes from the job title when it states one: Entry level (Apprentice, Trainee, Junior, Helper, New Grad, "Electrician 1", "Accountant I"), Mid-level ("II", Journeyman), Senior, Lead / Manager (Lead, Supervisor, Foreman, Charge Nurse, a manager of people), and up. A grade number only counts at the end of a title, and "Staff" means a senior rung only in engineering-style titles ("Staff Engineer"): a **Staff RN**, **Staff Accountant** or **IV Therapy Nurse** states no level. When the title says nothing, the years of experience the posting asks for decide between Entry (0–1), Mid (2–4) and Senior (5 or more), marked "(by years)". A job that states no level at all is never hidden by your level. When your level choice hides jobs, the count line above the list says so: **"12 hidden by your level"**.
  - **Remote** means the posting or its job system says so. "Not remote", "Non-remote", "Office Worker (NOT Remote)" and "Remote work is not available" are on-site; "Remote work is an option" is hybrid; a remote job tied to another country ("Remote, Poland", "… – Belfast, UK") is not a US-remote job and is left out, unless its location also names the US ("… – Asia" at "United States" is a US job that sells to Asia).
  - **Requirements:** a higher credential meets a lower one (an RN meets "LPN license", a BSN meets "Associate's in Nursing", a Master's meets "Bachelor's"), and a list of alternatives ("RN or LPN", "will consider other designations like LPN") is met by any one of them.
  - After an update of Siftwell that reads postings better, the listings already saved are read again once, from their saved text, the next time your board is rebuilt (no downloading, no Claude). Your ratings, saved jobs and dates stay as they are.
- **Filters** (the button next to the search box) has two parts:
  - **My search**, at the top: your home ZIP (or City, ST) and search radius, the ways of working you'd consider (on-site, hybrid, remote), remote jobs anywhere in the US, your travel limit, the lowest base salary you'd take (leave it blank for no floor, which is how a new profile starts), and your level (**Any level**, or Entry level up to VP / Executive). These decide which jobs reach your board, and they are saved with your profile, so they stay the same on every device. Each change saves by itself; Siftwell re-checks your board (no new listings) and the page refreshes itself a few seconds later, with Filters still open. **When you change your home, radius or ways of working**, the board also looks up the employers the Siftwell directory has for the new area and offers them right there ("128 more employers within 50 miles … **Add them**"), with how long checking them takes; **Remove the employers this added** takes them back. If a profile from an earlier version has no home ZIP yet, Siftwell first explains that a ZIP changes which jobs count as near you, and asks before saving it. Jobs above or below your level (more than one step away) and jobs that need more travel than your limit are left off. With Claude connected, jobs that newly appear get a quick check automatically (see below). None of these reset Claude's ratings. First-time setup asks the same questions.

## What Siftwell looks for

Siftwell keeps one short list per profile that says what kind of work you do. You can see and change all of it under **My profile → Job search → What Siftwell looks for**:

- **Your most recent job title.** This counts most. Setup asks for it (and fills it in from your resume when it can).
- **Job titles you're looking for:** the same job under the other names employers use for it, plus other titles you'd take. A dashed chip is a whole occupation ("Registered Nurses + 141 other names"): every name on it counts.
- **One step up:** titles you'd move up into. Senior, lead and supervisor versions of your own titles already count without being listed.
- **Related roles:** different jobs you could plausibly move into. They count for less than your own titles.
- **Skills and tools:** your own skills only: the ones on your profile, your certifications, and things you said yes to in Siftwell's questions. Each one found in a posting's text adds a little; one that nearly every posting mentions adds nothing.
- **Your field.**

Every entry is a chip: press **×** to remove it (a removed title or occupation stops counting altogether, also when another occupation lists the same name), or type in the box under a group and press **Add**. Changes save right away and your board re-checks itself.

**Where the list comes from.** It starts from your own profile (your most recent job, the job titles on your profile, your skills) and is widened with the **O*NET** occupation list from the U.S. Department of Labor, which comes with Siftwell and works offline: type "RN" and Siftwell also knows "Registered Nurse", "Staff Nurse", "Charge Nurse" and the occupations next door. It only does this when the occupation list names your title outright; for a title that fits many kinds of work ("Project Manager", "Teacher") it adds nothing rather than guess. Without Claude it also fills in **One step up** where the list has an obvious one ("Charge Nurse" for a nurse, "Office Director" for an office manager). That part needs no Claude. With Claude connected, reading your resume also fills in other names, step-up titles and related roles (in the same request that reads the resume, not an extra one), and after you type a new job title by hand Siftwell asks Claude once for ideas (one small request). **Ask Claude for more titles** does the same on demand: what it suggests is added to what is already there (nothing is replaced), and titles you removed are not suggested again. Updating Siftwell builds this list from what you already have, without asking Claude anything.

**What it is used for.**
- **The Relevant score** (0 to 100) on every job. Siftwell works it out itself, instantly, for every job. The posted title decides: Siftwell first drops what employers add after the title (the site, the unit, the shift, a requisition number: "Phlebotomist - Westerville Family Physicians" is a Phlebotomist job), then compares it with your list as whole phrases in order ("Nurse Practitioner - ICU" is not an "ICU Nurse"). Short forms count as the same title (RN and Registered Nurse). How high a job can score: your own most recent title 100; a title you search for 90; Claude's other names for your job 86; the job you held before 82 (older ones less); another name from the occupation list 78; one step up 72; a related role 60; the same kind of work 50; a related occupation 42; a title that only shares words with yours, at most 25. A longer posted title gets a little less than an exact one. The full score needs your skills in the posting text too; with none of them it is about a tenth lower, and skills alone (no title match) reach 25. So 80 or more is rare and means "this is my job", and a job that has nothing to do with your work scores near 0. Open a job to see the reason in one line.
- **Which job titles reach your board:** jobs whose titles match the list (whole words, so "RN" never matches "Intern"). With nothing on the list, every job title is shown. Profiles from earlier versions keep the title filter they had, plus any title they add here.
- **The order of your board:** without Claude, most relevant first, then Appeal. With Claude, Best bets (see above).
- **Which jobs Claude rates first:** the limited number of ratings per run goes to the most relevant jobs, not the best-paid ones.
- Changing the list **never resets a rating** you already have. It only changes the order, and which jobs are rated next.

**It learns a little from you.** Saving or applying to a job moves jobs with similar titles up a few points; **Not for me** moves similar titles down a few points. It learns only from the words of those titles that are not your own job titles (the specialty, the place), so hiding one "Registered Nurse" posting in the wrong state never pushes Registered Nurse jobs down. Never more than about ten points, the section lists what it learned from, and **Forget what was learned** clears it (your saved and hidden jobs stay as they are). **Start this list over** rebuilds the whole list from your profile.

**Pay in the Appeal score.** If you set a lowest base, pay is scored against it (a point for every $2,000 above it), as before. With no lowest base, pay is scored against the other jobs on your own board: the middle of your board is 50 and the best-paid jobs you see are near 100, whether your field pays by the hour or six figures.

**Big job sites.** On employers that use Workday, Siftwell reads the full text only for postings whose titles are on somebody's list. The rest still appear on a board with no title filter, with their title, place and link; Siftwell fetches the full text the moment you open one (**Show the full posting**) or just before Claude rates it.

## The Employers page

The **Employers** page (the database icon at the top; earlier versions called it the Catalog) is the household's shared list of every employer Siftwell watches and every job listing it has found. The line under its title says so. Everyone's board is built from it, so a change here updates everyone's board within a minute. On a phone it shows the list of sections first; tap one to open it.

- **Employers:** every employer with the kind of careers site it uses, whether it is one of your employers (or who else in your household watches it), when it was last checked, how many listings are open, and a status: **OK**; **Looks incomplete** (the last check returned far fewer jobs than before, so Siftwell kept the old ones open rather than trust it; if a job site keeps failing or coming back empty for two weeks, its old postings are closed); **Too many to read all** (the job site only shows its first few thousand jobs, or the employer used up the time Siftwell gives one employer per check, about four minutes, so that one slow site can't hold up everyone else; nothing was closed, what was read is kept, and the next check carries on); **Couldn't be read** (with the reason); **Paused**; or **Not checked yet**. Search and filter by status, careers site type, or **Your employers** / **Not yours**; the count above the list follows the filter. **Add an employer** takes a careers page link and whether it is for you or for everyone in the household. Each employer has **Watch** / **Stop watching** (adds it to your employers or takes it off, and the list and its count update at once; it stays on this page for the rest of the household either way), **Check now** (checks just that employer, a minute or so; it waits its turn if something else is running), **Pause** / **Resume** (a paused company isn't checked and its jobs leave the boards until you resume it) and **Remove** (tap twice; its listings are kept as closed, so adding it back later keeps their history). Under each name you also see its **company facts** status ("Facts looked up Nov 10, 2026", "No company facts yet", "Facts researched by hand", or "Facts: none found" with the date), and a **Look up facts** / **Refresh facts** button (see **Company facts** below).
- **Job listings:** every listing Siftwell has found, 50 per page, searchable by title, company or location, open or closed, with when it was first and last seen. Under each one you see who it currently passes the filters for and Claude's rating for them ("not rated yet", "Opus 82", and so on). **On someone's board** / **On <name>'s board** narrows the list to listings that made it onto a board.
- **Finding new jobs:** the history of every check for new jobs, board rebuild, re-rate and employer search (how many employers, new and closed listings, problems, and how long each step took). This is also where you choose **Find new jobs automatically** (off, while Siftwell is running, or even when it is closed) and its time, the same control as **Settings → Finding new jobs**, and where **Find new jobs now** checks every employer for everyone in the household. The line next to the button says how many employers that is and about how long it takes (worked out from the real list, not a fixed number). While it runs you see the same one plain progress line as everywhere else; the technical line-by-line output is folded away under **Details**, only here, and disappears when the run ends.
- **Ratings:** for each person, how many listings pass their filters, how many Claude has rated (and how many got a careful or best-judgment look), and how many are waiting for a rating or need a fresh one. **Open Re-rate for <name>** switches to that person and opens the board's Re-rate panel.

### Company facts

When you open a job, **About <company>** shows facts about the employer: who owns it, its value (market cap or latest valuation), funding, headcount, founding year, headquarters, what employees usually get as equity, recent layoffs, and its Glassdoor rating, each with a link to where it came from. The Glassdoor rating and layoffs also count toward the company part of **Appeal**.

Siftwell gathers these facts itself, using Claude (the quick Haiku model, searching the web), so **it uses your Claude plan** (a small amount per company: Claude reads a few web pages for each one).

- **Each time Siftwell finds new jobs:** after the listings are fetched and rated, Claude looks up facts for a few companies that are on someone's board, the ones with the best-ranked jobs first. Companies that are on nobody's board are not looked up. The progress line shows "Claude is looking up company facts". The boards are refreshed afterwards so Appeal and the company details use the new facts.
- **How many each time:** **Settings → Claude usage → Company facts each time** (10 unless you change it; 0 to 200, and 0 turns it off). Facts are shared by the whole household, so Siftwell uses the highest number any profile has set.
- **Refreshing:** each company is looked up again after about six months. If Claude can't find facts for a company, Siftwell waits two weeks before trying it again.
- **Right now, for one company:** **Employers → Employers** has a **Look up facts** button on each company (**Refresh facts** once it has some). It usually takes under a minute. If an update, re-rate or employer search is running, it asks you to try again when that finishes.
- **Hand-researched facts win:** companies whose facts were researched by hand (files in the `company_facts` folder of your data folder) keep those facts; Claude doesn't look them up, and they show "Facts researched by hand" with no button.
- Facts Claude looked up say so under **About <company>** with the date. They can be wrong or out of date, so check the source links before you rely on them.

Without Claude connected, nothing is looked up: this step is skipped, and the Employers page shows "Connect Claude to get company facts: Settings, then Get started" instead of the buttons. Privacy: Claude is sent only the company's name, its sector, and a few of its job titles and locations from its listings; nothing from your profile.

### Find employers near me

**Employers → Find employers near me** builds your company list for you. Type a location (it starts with your home ZIP and radius, if you set them), an optional focus (for example "program management" or "healthcare"), tick the kinds of employers to look for (big employers and headquarters, regional offices of national firms, hospitals and health systems, universities, government and contractors, nonprofits and associations, startups and tech, small and mid-size businesses, and remote-friendly employers in your field), and pick a size: small (about 30 employers), medium (about 80), large (about 150) or **Exhaustive** (as many as it can find; see below). Siftwell tells you roughly how long it takes (up to an hour for a large search) before you start.

For each kind of employer, Claude searches the web for employers and their careers pages; this uses your Claude plan. Siftwell itself then opens each careers page and checks whether it can read the listings, skipping companies you already have. Progress shows per kind of employer. The search runs in the background: you can leave the page or close it and come back, and **Stop** / **Resume** pick up where it left off (even after Siftwell restarts). It takes turns with updates and re-rates, so one may wait for the other.

When it's done you see the employers Siftwell can read, with their kind, location, job system and number of open jobs. Tick the ones you want (or **Add all readable**); they become your employers (or everyone's, if you choose that). Siftwell then fetches their jobs. Employers Siftwell can't read are listed separately (folded away), grouped by the job system their careers page uses (for example "3 use Taleo, 2 use their own custom careers site"), biggest group first, with the reason and their careers link, so you can look at them yourself and see which job systems would add the most employers if Siftwell could read them. Under each kind of employer the page counts how many were found, how many Siftwell can read, how many it can't, and how many you already have.

**Exhaustive search** keeps going until it runs out of new employers. For each kind of employer, Claude searches again and again, each time digging into something narrower: first published lists (state and county "largest employers" lists, chamber of commerce directories, hospital association member lists, school district and college lists, state agency directories, Built In for your city), then sub-types (for example clinics and specialty practices after hospitals, community colleges after universities, county and city agencies after government), then each sizeable city or town within your radius and its county. Each time it tells Claude which employers it already found (names only) and asks for more. A kind of employer stops once two searches in a row add fewer than 2 new employers Siftwell can read. Siftwell still checks every careers page itself, and never checks the same link twice in one search.

- **It uses a lot of your Claude plan and takes hours.** Before you start, the page shows how many Claude searches ("passes") it may take and a rough time (for example "2 hours to 4 hours" for a big metro).
- **Budget:** you choose the most Claude passes it may use (40 to start with, between 5 and 200). The page shows "pass 12 of up to 40" while it runs. When it reaches the budget it stops; raise the budget next to **Resume** to keep going.
- **Stop** / **Resume** work the same as for the other sizes: it remembers what it already found and carries on from there, even after Siftwell restarts. A pass that was stopped half way is done again when you resume (and counts towards the budget again). Between passes it lets updates and re-rates run.

**Start my job search** (the end of setup, or the card on an empty board) runs a medium search for your home ZIP and radius only when you tick **Find more employers with Claude** there, and adds every employer Siftwell can read to your employers, together with employers it found that Siftwell already had. It shows up here like any other search. Nothing in Siftwell starts a Claude employer search by itself.

Find employers needs Claude Code installed and signed in on this computer. If it isn't, the page says so and links to **Settings → Get started**.

Privacy: for its web searches, Find employers sends Claude the location you typed (your home ZIP, for **Start my job search**) and your focus, if any, plus the names of companies that Siftwell already has so it can skip them (an Exhaustive search also sends the names of the employers it has found so far, and the cities and towns in your area). For the **Remote-friendly employers in my field** kind it also sends your target roles and the career direction from your profile. Nothing else from your profile or resume is sent.

### Sharing companies and listings

**Employers → Share** lets you pass your company list to another Siftwell household (a friend in the same city, say), or receive theirs.

- **Export:** tick **All of your employers** or pick companies, choose whether to include their current listings (the open job posts and their text), and click **Download .siftwell file**. The file only holds public information: company names, their job systems and, if ticked, their open listings. It never contains profiles, resumes, saved or applied marks, tailored resumes, settings, your access key, Claude's ratings, company research or Glassdoor facts, or who added what.
- **Import:** choose a `.siftwell` file. Siftwell shows what it holds ("312 new companies, 9,840 new listings, 41 that Siftwell already has"), you pick the list to put the new companies in, and click **Add**. Companies you already have are matched by their job board, listings by their id; nothing you have is removed or closed, and each listing keeps the earliest "first seen" and latest "last seen" date. Everyone's boards are rebuilt afterwards. Siftwell checks everything in the file first: companies with a job system it doesn't know or with settings that look wrong (for example a link to a private network address) are skipped, links that aren't ordinary web links are dropped, and dates in the future are set to today.

## The Siftwell employer directory

The **Siftwell employer directory** is a free list of employers across the US whose job pages Siftwell can read, with the places they hire and how many openings they have. **Start my job search** uses it to find your employers in seconds, so your board has employers near you from day one without Claude.

- **Free, no keys, no account, no Claude.** It's rebuilt every week by an automatic job on GitHub (the project's own GitHub repository, branch `index`) from public sources: web-crawl indexes (Common Crawl) and public certificate logs, which show which companies use the job systems Siftwell reads (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS and others); lists of employers' own websites that carry job postings (Web Data Commons); and the job feeds themselves, read once a week to see where each employer hires. An employer with several career sites (for example a university's staff jobs and its student jobs) is listed once per site, so none of its jobs are missed. Only employers with open jobs are listed. The directory never reads a job site whose own rules (robots.txt) ask crawlers to stay away.
- **Names.** Some job systems only know an employer by a code or a run-together web address. The directory looks up the real name from the employer's job page and postings; when it can't, Siftwell shows a plain description instead of the code, for example **Employer in Granville OH (UKG lic100)**. The first time Siftwell checks that employer's openings it reads the name from the postings themselves ("Cameronmitchell" becomes **Cameron Mitchell**, a leading accounting code is dropped, "Juvenile Justice, Department of" becomes **Department of Juvenile Justice**) and renames the employer. Nothing is lost when that happens: its jobs keep their ratings, your saved and applied marks and their first-seen dates. A name you typed yourself when adding a company is never changed.
- **Job postings from other sources.** When the project has set them up, the directory also brings postings from **USAJOBS** (federal jobs in every state), **SchoolSpring** (school jobs) and the monthly Hacker News **Who is hiring** thread; **Settings → Employer directory** names the sources in use. They show up on your board like any other job, under a company named after the source (for example **Federal jobs (USAJOBS)**), go through your filters and ratings as usual, name the actual employer in the job details, and link to the original posting, where you apply.
- **Privacy:** Siftwell downloads whole-state files only (for example the file for Ohio, plus any neighbouring state within your radius), and a remote-jobs file if you only want remote work. Your ZIP code and exact location are never sent anywhere; the matching happens on your computer. The files contain only company names, job-system settings, careers links, places with job counts and dates, nothing about people.
- **Where it's kept:** in a `directory` folder inside your Siftwell data folder. Siftwell checks for a new edition at most once a week and keeps working from the copy it has when you're offline.
- **Nothing is added blindly.** **Settings → Employer directory** shows where it looks (your home ZIP and radius, from **Filters → My search**), when the directory was last updated, and, before you add anything, **how many employers** it would add, **about how many openings** they have near you, and **how long** the first check and the daily look for new jobs would take (worked out from the real number of employers and how long each one took last time). Choose **all** of them or only **the ones with the most openings near you**, then press **Add … employers**. Siftwell starts checking their openings, the ones with the most openings near you first; your board fills in as it goes (one line at the top shows "Checking employers: 184 of 1,043", with **Pause**), and if the check is interrupted the board offers **Continue checking**. Changed your mind? **Remove the employers added …** takes the last batch off again (their jobs leave your board; you can add them again later). In a household, the daily look for new jobs checks each employer once for everyone, and each person's own **Find new jobs** checks only their own employers.
- **When you change where you look** (home, radius, or ways of working, in **Filters → My search**), your board offers the directory's employers for the new area right away, with the count and the same choice. You don't have to come to Settings.
- **No home ZIP (profiles from an earlier version).** If your profile still uses Siftwell's built-in Washington, DC area list, **Settings → Employer directory** lets you give the directory its own place and radius. That only chooses which employers are added; which jobs count as near you stays exactly as it is until you set a home ZIP in **Filters → My search**.
- **Big employers Siftwell isn't sure about.** A few very large job sites are too big for the directory to read completely, so it can't always tell whether they hire near you. Siftwell adds those too when they hire somewhere in your state, checks them once, and takes the ones with no jobs near you (and no remote jobs, if you're open to remote work) off your **Near me** list again.
- **A city name without its state.** "Columbus" is a city in Ohio, Georgia, Indiana and more. When a posting gives only a city name like that (or a name with the city inside it, such as "Columbus Healthcare Center"), Siftwell places it only when something backs one state: the posting itself names the state ("a current Georgia license"), the employer's other postings put a town of that name in one state, or the employer is known to hire in just one of them. When nothing points at any of the towns with that name, the one inside your own area counts (a "Bowie" posting shows for someone near Bowie, Maryland). When what is known points somewhere else, or at several of them at once, the posting is treated as one without a clear location (next point) instead of being guessed into your area: "Columbus" at an employer known in Georgia never shows up near Columbus, Ohio. A place written with its state ("Dublin, OH") is never in doubt.
- **Jobs without a clear location.** Some postings only say "Main Office", "Headquarters", "Statewide" or nothing at all. When every place the directory knows for that employer is inside your radius, Siftwell treats such a posting as local and shows it as, for example, "Main Office (near Columbus, OH)". Locations given as a county ("Franklin County, OH") are understood as that county.
- **Staffing agencies** from the directory are labelled **Staffing agency** on the Employers page, so you can pause or remove them if you'd rather not see their postings.
- **Weekly:** once a week Siftwell also adds every employer newly listed near you on its own; their openings are checked by the next **Find new jobs** (or **Continue checking**). If you chose only the ones with the most openings, nothing is added by itself. An employer you stop watching (or remove on the Employers page) is never added back.
- **If the directory can't be reached:** Siftwell tells you so, in plain words, wherever it matters. On your **first search** (and only then) it falls back to a starter list of about 200 large US employers that comes with the app, so you still get a board: the ones known to hire in your state are checked first, then the biggest, and after their first check the ones with no jobs near you (or no remote jobs, if you're open to remote work) are taken off again and never added back that way. Setup, your board and **Settings → Employer directory** all say that this is the starter list and not the employers near you, and offer **Try the directory again**. Pressing **Add employers** while the directory can't be reached adds nothing and says so. The same starter list fills in when the directory has no file for your state yet.
- **Want more?** **Employers → Find employers near me** asks Claude to search the web for employers the directory doesn't have. It uses your Claude plan and only runs when you start it.

## Using Siftwell without Claude

Claude is an optional upgrade. Without it Siftwell still fetches listings, filters them to your location, pay and level, and ranks them by how **relevant** they are to your work, then by **Appeal**.

**What works without Claude**
- **Start my job search** (it adds employers near you from the free Siftwell employer directory, then checks their career pages), **Settings → Employer directory**, finding new jobs automatically (if you turn it on), the board, the **Relevant** and **Appeal** scores (the board is ordered by how relevant each job is to your work, then by Appeal; with no job title given, by Appeal), every filter, Save / Applied / Not for me, the Companies tab and the Employers page.
- **Search** the board by typing words and pressing Enter: it matches every word against the title, company, place and posting text, with a one-line note that this is a plain keyword search.
- **Fill in my profile myself:** on **My profile → Resume** (and in the first-time setup) a short form takes your name, location, current title, summary, roles, skills, education, certifications and clearance and makes your profile. A Word (`.docx`) or text (`.txt`) resume can pre-fill the form (it is read on your computer, no AI); you check everything before saving. Scanned PDFs, photos and Google Doc links need Claude.

**What needs Claude**
- **Match** ratings, the verdicts and **Claude's read** on each job, the **Re-rate** panel and automatic rating after updates (these quietly skip).
- **Tailor my resume**, the Astra review, tailoring questions, **Target** suggestions and ratings, reading a resume with Claude, and plain-English search that understands what you mean.
- Looking up **company facts** (skipped quietly; facts already gathered still show).
- **Find employers near me** (Claude's web search; also the optional **Find more employers with Claude** box in **Start my job search**). The free employer directory doesn't need Claude.

Texts that only make sense with Claude are left out (for example the note about a quick check of new jobs, or "Tap Tailor my resume" on the empty Resumes tab, which says tailoring needs Claude instead). Where something needs Claude, Siftwell shows one note ("Connect Claude to get Match ratings and tailoring → Settings → Get started") and a dash where the Match number would be, instead of an error. Once you connect Claude, upload your resume and Claude reads it to improve the profile you filled in. You can connect Claude during first-time setup (the **Connect Claude** box on the resume step) or any time later in **Settings → Get started**.

## Your data stays on your computer

Your information stays on this computer. Siftwell keeps your resume, profiles, saved jobs and ratings in a folder on your PC (not in the cloud). If you connect Claude, job descriptions and your resume are sent to Claude only to rate jobs and tailor resumes. Everything Siftwell creates or learns is stored only on your computer, outside the app folder:
(**Settings → About & updates** shows exactly where; the folder names are in [More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md#where-siftwell-keeps-things).)

That includes resumes, profiles, settings, saved and applied jobs, tailored resumes, the companies and job listings it has found, Claude's ratings, your phone access key, and logs. The Siftwell folder itself only holds the app; Siftwell never saves anything into it.

When Siftwell moves to a newer way of storing something, it puts the old files in an `_archive` folder inside your data folder and removes them after 30 days.

So you can delete the Siftwell folder and download the latest version (or move it) and pick up exactly where you left off. Claude's ratings are tied to your own information (resume, answers, goals and target), not to the app version, so a new version doesn't re-rate anything or spend your Claude plan on ratings you already have. They're only redone when you change your own information. Job descriptions and your resume are sent to Claude (and to ChatGPT if you turn on Astra) to rate and tailor; nothing is sent anywhere else.

## Updating Siftwell

Siftwell looks for a newer version on the project's GitHub **Releases** page: once a day (when it starts, then daily) and whenever you click **Settings → About & updates → Check for updates**. Switch the daily look off with **Check for updates automatically** in the same place; **About & updates** also shows the version you're running. If the page can't be reached (no internet, or the project's page is private), Siftwell says nothing.

When a newer version exists, a small bar appears on the board and in Settings: **Siftwell 0.3.0 is available. What's new · Update**. **What's new** shows the release notes as plain text. **Dismiss** hides the bar for that version (**About & updates** still shows it).
- **Installed with the Windows installer:** click **Update**. Siftwell downloads **Siftwell-Setup-x.y.z.exe** into the `_updates` folder inside your data folder, checks it arrived whole (size and, when GitHub lists one, its checksum), then runs it quietly in the background, closes, and opens again by itself when the new version is in. Your profiles, ratings and settings stay as they are. If the download or the install fails, Siftwell shows why and keeps running the version you have. It won't start while Siftwell is finding new jobs or re-rating; try again when that finishes.
- **Installed as a Mac app (from the .dmg):** click **Update**. Siftwell downloads **Siftwell-x.y.z.dmg** into the `_updates` folder inside your data folder, checks it arrived whole (size and, when GitHub lists one, its checksum), copies the new Siftwell next to the old one and checks it, then quits, swaps the two and opens the new one by itself. Your data stays as it is, and macOS doesn't ask about the new version again. If Siftwell can't replace itself (it isn't in a folder you can write to, or you are running it straight from the disk image instead of from Applications) it says why and shows **Download the new version**; then update by hand: download the .dmg and drag Siftwell onto **Applications**, choosing **Replace**. A log of the swap is in `_updates/mac-update.log`.
- **Portable version or the ZIP:** Siftwell shows a download link instead of updating itself; the steps are in [More options & troubleshooting](ADVANCED.md#updating-the-portable-version-or-the-zip).

## Moving to another computer / backups

Siftwell can pack your data into one file and unpack it on any computer, so it doubles as a backup. What is in it, and what isn't, is listed below.

**Make a backup:** open **Settings → Backup & move** and click **Export everything**. After a short wait (a minute or so for a big board) your browser saves `Siftwell-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.zip`. It holds every profile with its resume, settings, saved and applied marks and tailored resumes, Claude's ratings, the employers and job listings, company facts, the employer directory's memory (which employers you were offered, added and removed, so a removed one stays removed and newly listed ones keep arriving), the history behind **Settings → Claude usage**, your schedule, phone-access setting and access key. It leaves out logs, temporary files, the window's size and position, downloaded app updates, whether Siftwell starts when you sign in (set that again on the new computer), your Claude and ChatGPT sign-ins (sign in again there) and your Anthropic API key (type it in again). Profiles you removed are only included if you tick **Include removed profiles**. If Siftwell is finding new jobs or re-rating, Siftwell asks you to try again when it finishes, so nothing is saved half-written.

**This file contains your resumes and personal details. Keep it private** (don't email it or put it somewhere public).

**Move to another computer:**
1. Install Siftwell on the new computer (see **Install**) and start it.
2. On the welcome page click **Bring over my data from another computer** and choose the backup file. (If you've already set up a profile there, use **Settings → Backup & move → Restore from a backup** instead.)
3. Siftwell shows what's inside: who, how many jobs, ratings and tailored resumes, and which Siftwell version made it. Click **Restore**, then **Open my board**.

Restoring onto a computer that already has data **replaces everything currently in Siftwell on that computer**, so it asks you to tap twice. Before replacing anything it moves the current data to a folder named `before-restore-<date and time>` inside the `_archive` folder in your data folder, so you can get it back by hand. Siftwell checks the file first and refuses anything that isn't a Siftwell backup or isn't safe to open. A backup made by a newer Siftwell restores with a warning; one from a newer backup format asks you to update Siftwell first.

After a restore the boards refresh by themselves (no restart). Your phone link keeps the same key but your new computer's network address may differ: if phone access is on, the new link is shown after the restore (you can also scan the QR code again in **Settings → Get started**). Claude's ratings come back with the backup; a restore never starts Claude by itself. Jobs that aren't rated yet get rated the next time Siftwell finds new jobs or when you re-rate.

## Good to know

- **Claude usage:** ratings and company facts run on your Claude plan. **Settings → Claude usage** starts with **What Siftwell has used**: a small table of the tokens, requests and jobs for today and the last 7 days, by feature (Match ratings, Target ratings, Tailored resumes and so on). It is counted from what Claude itself reports after each request Siftwell makes on this computer, and looking at it never uses Claude. A request that brought nothing back (it timed out, or the answer couldn't be read) is listed too, as "got no answer". Below it, **How much Claude rates in one run** has four numbers: **Quick checks per run** (up to 500), **Careful look: best % of rated jobs** (at most 100 jobs per run), **Best judgment: your top jobs** (at most 50 new per run, plus up to 25 jobs you saved) and **Target-tab ratings per run** (up to 50). A run is one automatic rating after Find new jobs, one scheduled night (the automatic Find new jobs and the re-rate after it share one run's limits), or one press of Rate more or Re-rate; the limits are the same for all of them and you can lower them, not raise them. (If you saved larger numbers in an earlier version, they now simply mean "as many as allowed".) The page also sets how many companies get their facts looked up each time. Large first-time ratings can take a while and use a noticeable share of your plan's limits. If a new version shows many ratings as needing a fresh rating even though you haven't changed your resume or answers, press **Keep my current ratings** on that page (two taps) to mark them as up to date instead of re-rating them; it doesn't use Claude.
- **Job sites:** Siftwell reads the public job listings employers publish on their career sites, at a polite pace, for your personal job search. Use it responsibly and respect each site's terms.
